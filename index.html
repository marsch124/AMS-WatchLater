How this works
Four ways to get a video in. They all end up in the same list, so use whichever is nearest to hand.

1 — Add to WatchLater, from Raycast or Alfred. With a video open in Safari, type its name and press Return. Nothing moves, nothing opens, the page stays exactly where it was; a notification tells you what was saved. This is the one to build the habit around.
2 — A hotkey, if you want one. Raycast can give any app a key combination in its own settings — pick Add to WatchLater there and press the keys you want. Nothing else to install.
3 — Paste. Drop one link or fifty into the box at the top, one per line, and press Add. This is how you empty a window full of tabs in one go.
4 — Drag and drop. Pull a link from anywhere onto this window.

The row of buttons is the whole idea. You almost never want to know what you saved on Tuesday. You want to know what fits in the twenty minutes you actually have. Click a time and the list shows only videos that fit inside it. Everything shows the lot. That one change is what turns a saved list into something you open.

The row also tells you how much is there. Beside the times you get, for whatever is showing, something like 9 videos · 2 h 40 min. It is the difference between “a few things saved” and “that is a whole evening” — and it is usually the sentence that makes you either pick one now or clear a couple out. A + at the end means one of them has no length yet, so the real total is a little higher.

A line about why you saved it. Under the title of every card there is room for one line in your own words — for the trip, the bit about sleep, watch with Lisa. Click it and type; Return saves, Escape forgets it. Three weeks later the title alone rarely tells you what you were after, and that is exactly when a video gets skipped over instead of watched. It is one line on the card on purpose — nothing here deserves a screen of its own.

Pinning. The pin in the corner of a picture holds that video at the top of the list, above everything else, until you press it again. For the one or two you have actually decided to watch next, rather than the pile you might.

Lengths arrive on their own. When a link comes in, the length is read straight off the YouTube page, here on this Mac. There is no account, no key and nothing to set up. If a video has been made private or taken down the length cannot be read, and rather than saving a blank card the app tells you it could not read the link. For anything that is not YouTube, the card asks you to tap a rough length so it still lands in the right time bucket.

Nothing rots quietly. Anything sitting untouched for three months turns amber and asks whether you still want it. Keep resets its clock for another three months; the bin removes it. Without this, a read-later list only ever grows, and a list that only grows is one you stop opening. That is what happened to every other one you have tried.

Ticking one off. The tick marks a video watched and takes it out of the list. Watched in the button row brings them back into view if you want to find something a second time.

Nothing asks “are you sure”. Tick something by mistake, or bin the wrong card, and a small message appears at the bottom with Undo for five seconds. A question you have to answer every single time stops being read after the first week and catches nothing; five seconds of undo actually does. While that offer stands the video is only marked, not gone — so even closing this window mid-undo cannot lose it.

The engine. A small program runs quietly in the background to do the lookups and hold the list. You never have to start it: it comes up when the Mac starts, and both apps start it themselves if it is ever not running. The first video of the day may take a second or two longer while it wakes up.
Your data
Everything lives in one file on this Mac — watchlater.json, in the AMS WatchLater folder. Thumbnails sit next to it in thumbs, so pictures still show with the internet off.

A dated copy is written into the backups folder every time the list changes, and the last fourteen days are kept. That copy refuses to overwrite a fuller one from the same day, so a bad moment cannot quietly replace a good list with an empty one. To go back, put a dated file in place of watchlater.json.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere and there is no account. The only thing that ever leaves this Mac is the lookup that fetches a video's title and length.
